Translation Notes

  1. ↑ Izumi actually compares Tsukasa to umeboshi (dried brined ume fruits), which I think is a dish known outside of Japan, but I substituted it because this is a common phrasing in English.
  2. ↑ This is in reference to a line said by the character Muska in the Ghibli movie Castle in the Sky.
  3. ↑ ES! continuity: this is in reference to Trickstar’s performance at the end of the ES! Main Story, in which they won against fine and earned the right to compete in that year's SS: Part 5 - Ensemble. This was Trickstar’s first major victory and happened a little over two years before the present story.
  4. ↑ Tsukasa for some reason calls Ritsu “Sakuma-senpai” instead of “Ritsu-senpai” only for this single line.
  5. ↑ Since I’ve already committed to not adapting ucchuu: while uchuu means “space”, daiuchuu (same as uchuu but with the character for “big” at the start) means “macrocosm” or “the cosmos”. As shown in Opera, “daiucchuu” is the original version of Leo’s “ucchuu” catchphrase and is suggested to have originated from Ensemble Girls! character Ruri Amamiya. “Daiucchuu” can potentially be interpreted as a more intense or pronounced version of “ucchuu”.
  6. ↑ Leo’s “repeat after me” is rendered in katakana while “how are you?” is in the Latin alphabet. The former can be interpreted as him copying Tsukasa rather than attempting to speak proper English, while the latter is written the same as Tsukasa’s own English.
  7. ↑ Izumi says ”ore wa Leo-kun ni…”. This phrasing implies that he was about to say something relating to his thoughts, feelings or desires in regards to Leo.
